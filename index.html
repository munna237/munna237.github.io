Reconstructing MRI from less data than the scanner is supposed to need.

Deep learning normally pays for accuracy with labelled data that clinics in Bangladesh do not have. My work asks how far self-supervised and label-efficient methods can close that gap — in MRI reconstruction, in histopathology, and in screening tools meant to run on ordinary hospital hardware.

Research

What I work on

My undergraduate thesis, Self-Supervised Learning for Offline MRI Reconstruction, trains reconstruction models without fully sampled ground truth — the setting that actually holds in hospitals where nobody has time to acquire reference scans. Around it sit related questions: how much image compression a diagnostic model can survive, how to squeeze a multi-task diagnostic pipeline onto a small device, and how to audit a model well enough that a clinician could reasonably trust it.

Reconstruction
Self-supervised and offline MRI reconstruction from undersampled k-space.
Label efficiency
Contrastive and self-supervised pretraining where annotation budgets are small.
Clinical robustness
Compression thresholds, distribution shift, and failure auditing in diagnostic models.
Trustworthy AI
Explainable attention and error analysis for screening tools in low-resource settings.
